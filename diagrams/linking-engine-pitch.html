How it works

Two jobs, not one

Most linking tools only suggest new links. On a site that's been running for years, the bigger win is usually hiding in the links you already have.

It audits what's there

Every existing link gets scored. Does the anchor text describe the page it points to? Is the link buried in a footer when it belongs in the copy? Is it broken, redirected, or pointing somewhere that no longer matters? A page with forty inbound links all saying "read more" is losing something on every one of them.

It finds what's missing

Then it reads the meaning of every page and the structure of the whole site, and works out which pages should be connected but aren't. New pages that nothing points to. Guides that cover the same topic without acknowledging each other. Pages sitting three clicks deeper than they deserve.

Anchor text

It doesn't write your copy. It finds the words already in it.

The clickable text of a link tells search engines what the destination page is about. "Click here" tells them nothing. But rewriting a client's content to insert keywords is the wrong fix, and it's what most automated tools do.

This engine looks for a phrase already sitting in your page that describes the target, and links that instead. Nothing is invented.

What to expect

What it does and doesn't promise

  • It ranks opportunities by how valuable they're likely to be. It does not predict how much traffic any single link will bring, because that isn't honestly knowable.
  • It learns from you. Every suggestion you accept, edit, or dismiss trains the ranking, so the queue matches your judgement more closely over time.
  • It shows its reasoning. Every recommendation lists the signals behind it and flags its own assumptions.
  • It runs when your content changes, not on a timer. Nothing recomputes while your site sits still.
  • A person still decides. Nothing is published automatically.
